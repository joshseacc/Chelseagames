Choose a comparison scenario to see how the meaning changes:
Simple Past: The Closed Door Finished Time
"I did it."

The action was completed at a specific moment in the past. It is an isolated fact in history. It makes no claim about the present state.

🚫 Key Rule: Use when time is stated or implied (yesterday, in 2020, ago).
Present Perfect: The Live Bridge Reaches NOW
"I have done it."

Even though it started earlier, the result or importance exists right NOW. You are sharing this information because it matters to the present moment.

✅ Key Rule: Never attach a finished specific time like "yesterday" or "in 2019".
💡

The 1-Second Deciding Test:

Ask yourself: "Does the speaker care about WHEN it happened, or THE FACT THAT IT MATTERS NOW?"
• If WHEN matters (or time is finished) → Simple Past.
• If NOW matters (result/experience/still true) → Present Perfect.

Tense 1 of 5

Simple Present

Things that are always true, regular habits, or fixed schedules. It is not about this exact split second, but about what holds true in general.

Simple Present: General Routine / Fact

"I drink coffee every morning."

Does not mean I am drinking coffee right now. It describes my habitual life pattern.

Contrast with Present Continuous

"I am drinking coffee right now."

Active at this exact moment; I have a mug in my hand right now.

Select a scenario to view on timeline:
Example: "I play tennis every Saturday." Repeated pattern across Past, Present, and Future
Example: "Water boils at 100°C." A timeless truth that was, is, and always will be valid
Example: "The train leaves at 9:00 PM tonight." Simple present used for official scheduled future events
💡
Quick Test: Ask: "Is this a habit, a fact, or an official schedule?"
• "I work in banking." (Simple Present → My permanent job).
• "I am working on a report." (Present Continuous → What I am typing right now).

Tense 2 of 5

Simple Past

The "Closed Chapter" tense. The action started, happened, and completely concluded in the past. There is zero overlap with Now.

Simple Past: Finished Time & Action

"I lived in London for 3 years."

Implies: I do NOT live in London today. It is a finished episode in my past history.

Contrast with Present Perfect

"I have lived in London for 3 years."

Implies: I still live in London right now today! The period is still open.

Select a scenario to view on timeline:
Example: "I visited Paris in 2022." Clear time stamp; completely isolated from Now
Example: "I walked to school every day as a kid." Repeated in the past, but completely stopped before today
Example: "He entered the room, turned on the light, and sat down." Chronological step-by-step narrative in past
💡
Quick Test: Does the sentence specify when in the past (e.g. yesterday, in 2019, 5 minutes ago)? If yes, use Simple Past! You cannot attach specific past times to Present Perfect.

Tense 3 of 5

Present Continuous

The "Live Camera" tense. An action is actively in progress right now, or is a temporary situation happening around this time.

Present Continuous: Temporary / Dynamic

"I am staying with my friend this week."

Temporary situation; it started recently and will end soon.

Contrast with Simple Present

"I live with my parents."

Permanent baseline truth; regular long-term living situation.

Select a scenario to view on timeline:
Example: "I am studying English right now." The action is active at this exact instant
Example: "He is looking for a new apartment this month." Not necessarily happening at this second, but true for this short period
Example: "I am having dinner with Sarah tomorrow." Personal arrangement already finalized today in my calendar
💡
Quick Test: Imagine looking through a live phone camera. If you can see the person in motion doing the activity right now, use Present Continuous!

Tense 4 of 5

Past Continuous

The "Background Scene" tense. Describes an ongoing action in progress at a specific moment in the past, often interrupted by another event.

Past Continuous + Simple Past

"I was taking a shower when the phone rang."

Shower = ongoing background wave. Phone ringing = sudden sharp interruption.

Contrast with Two Simple Pasts

"When the phone rang, I answered it."

Consecutive actions: first the phone rang, and then I picked it up.

Select a scenario to view on timeline:
Example: "I was studying when my friend arrived." Wave = ongoing action; Pin = interrupting event (Simple Past)
Example: "While I was cooking, my sister was doing homework." Two ongoing continuous actions running side by side
Example: "At 8:00 PM yesterday, I was watching a film." Looking directly into the middle of an in-progress past event
💡
Quick Test: Was the action like a movie playing in the background ("I was walking") when a sudden photo snap clicked ("I slipped")? Background = Past Continuous, Snapshot = Simple Past.

Tense 5 of 5

Present Perfect

The "Live Bridge" tense. Why is it called "Present" if the action began earlier? Because its result, impact, or life experience belongs strictly to NOW.

Simple Past: "The Closed Book"

"I lost my keys yesterday."

Historical report. Do I have them now? Maybe I found them an hour ago, maybe not. The sentence doesn't guarantee current state.

Present Perfect: "The Live Bridge to Now"

"I have lost my keys!"

The real message is: "I don't have them right now, so I'm locked out outside!"

Select a scenario to view on timeline:
Example: "I have broken my glasses." Result right now: Can't see clearly at this exact moment
Example: "I have visited Japan twice." Timeline = My open life experience up to this moment
Example: "I have lived here for 10 years." Started in the past, still living here today!
💡
Quick Test: Ask yourself: "Does the speaker care about WHEN it happened, or the FACT that it matters right now?" If it directly affects the present moment, choose Present Perfect!
